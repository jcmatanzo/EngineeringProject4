Assignment 8: Prototyping & Design for Manufacturing

Lumetriq already has a concept (Week 6) and an economic model that works over 5 years (Week 7). This week defines which assumption to test first and with which prototype, reports on the prototype we have already built, applies the 5-step DFM process to the Fixed Reflex pod (Ø 160 × 77 mm), and reviews it against Mexico's General Law on the Circular Economy.

How to read this page: version from November 25, 2026. Prices in MXN, per pod and in batches of 100, matching the Week 7 BOM ($1,000 per pod, $4,000 per 4-pod package). [Estimated] = our own calculation that still needs to be measured; [Verify] = data to be confirmed with the supplier or at the official source; [To Fill In] = evidence captured in this week's tests. An assembly hour is valued at $77.32: the salary of the employee in the business case ($10,000 per month ÷ 174.6 h × 1.35 for benefits).

5. Prototype Report

Function Prototype Already Built: The 18-LED Arc

This prototype was built before this class and answered a question that the battery design depended on. Since it is a function prototype, the evidence is a measurement, not an observation of users.

PROTOTYPE REPORT — Week 8
Team: Lumetriq · Concept: Fixed Reflex

PROTOTYPE TYPE: Function
RESOLUTION: Low (loose strip, no housing or diffuser)
MATERIALS AND TIME: an 18-LED WS2812B segment (144 LED/m strip),
a ≈ 5 V power supply, and a multimeter in series. Less than 1 h.

QUESTION IT ANSWERED:
Can a single 18650 cell power the 18-LED arc directly, without a
boost converter, and deliver the 3 h of use required by the PDS (PR-01)?

EVIDENCE COLLECTED (a measurement, not an opinion):
· 18 LEDs in cyan at 100% (green + blue), at 4.927 V: 0.326 A (1.61 W).
· This is the worst case: ≈ 18 mA per LED, two channels at maximum.
· With the PDS profile (40% brightness, on 30% of the time), the
  arc averages ≈ 0.04 A [Estimated].

WAS THE ASSUMPTION VALIDATED? Partially.
Why: the draw is low for an 18650, and the calculated battery life
is ≈ 5.7 h with the 1,800 mAh cell (see test T4 in Week 9). We still
need to check that blue and white look right at 3.5–3.7 V, which is
what the cell delivers when it is almost empty.

KEY LEARNING:
The pod's real load is the LEDs and the ESP32. The sensors add up to
less than 3 mA.

DESIGN CHANGE:
A single 18650; the LEDs run directly from the battery, and only the
ESP32 goes through the MT3608 boost converter at 5 V. Low-battery
warning at 3.55 V and shutdown at 3.40 V.

NEXT HYPOTHESIS TO PROTOTYPE:
The price: will a gym owner leave their details after seeing $6,000?

Volume: 100 pods (25 packages), the BOM batch. The output was checked against the project's current decisions, and assembly times were estimated operation by operation.

What this review tells the business case: Week 7 uses $1,000 in components per pod and a payroll that, spread out, comes to ≈ $60 per pod in year 2. 9. Circular Economy

The Pod Against the General Law on the Circular Economy (DOF, January 19, 2026)

QuestionPrincipleCurrent StateDesign Change
Are the components accessible for repair?Repairability🟢 Yes for the housing (screws and inserts) and the pad (cover with a tension ring). 🟡 No for the battery: the 18650 has soldered tabsAn 18650 battery holder (+$15 per pod) so the cell can be replaced without soldering
Can the modules be replaced separately?Modularity🟢 Yes: the DevKit and ADS1220 sit on female headers; the load cells have pins. 🟡 No: the ADXL375 has soldered wires, and the load cells are glued with epoxyA connector for the ADXL375; a printed support that holds the load cell without glue
Are the materials identified for recycling?Life cycle🔴 No: no part is marked. The faux leather is PVC, which is hard to recycleEmboss ">PETG<" on the body, bezel, and ring when printing; mark the steel; look for a PVC-free skin for v2
Can it be updated without being replaced?Timelessness🔴 No: the firmware is loaded through the DevKit's USB port, inside the podOTA updates over BLE from the app (the ESP32 supports this with two partitions)

Extended Producer Responsibility: What Lumetriq Would Do

  • Take-back: at the end of its life, the gym returns the pods and gets a discount on a new package.
  • Recovery: the steel plates (≈ 1.05 kg per pod), modules, and PCB are tested and reused; the battery goes to a battery collection center.
  • Spare parts: the pad cover, battery, and diffuser are sold separately; the pod is not replaced because of a $5–10 part.
  • When it applies: EPR will be applied through SEMARNAT agreements by sector, and electronic products are among the first. Designing for it today avoids a redesign later.

13. Reflection and Key Takeaways

This week we realized that Lumetriq's most urgent prototype is not the pod. When we reviewed the blueprint, the blocks with the most uncertainty were all about the business: we have never spoken with the gym owner, who is the one who pays, and we don't know whether anyone will spend $6,000 on 4 pods. Building the perfect pod before knowing that would repeat the mistake described in class: reaching the end with something polished that nobody tested. That is why our first prototype is the landing page with the real price and a form, and the evidence we are after is not whether people like it, but whether they leave their details. We also noticed that we had already been prototyping without calling it that: measuring the LED arc answered a concrete question and changed the battery design.

The DFM review surprised us the most. We assumed the cost was in the components, but of the pod's 119 mechanical parts, 94 are screws, nuts, washers, and standoffs, and the estimated 4 hours of assembly cost almost as much as the accelerometer. That also exposed a gap in the business case: the planned payroll is not enough to assemble 2,000 pods a year with the current design.

Finally, the circular economy law made us see that, although the pod can be opened, the soldered battery, the unmarked materials, and firmware that can only be loaded from the inside make it a disposable product. These are cheap changes if we make them now, and much more expensive once the design is frozen.
