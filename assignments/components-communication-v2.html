Component List v2: Impact Sensing and Impact-Resistant Structure

Supplement to the technical design and the critical review of the boxing pod · Lookup date: October 8, 2026

Tags: [Confirmed] = verified at the stated source. [Quoted] = price the team already had. [Estimated] = our own calculation or range. [Hypothesis] = assumption to be validated on the test bench. [Pending] = still to be verified.

What does not change: cylindrical pod Ø140 × 75 mm (Week 6 Fixed Reflex concept; previously Ø130 × 70), ESP32-WROOM-32E, ADXL375 as the direct acceleration measurement, BLE gateway + ESP-NOW, stacking with a recessed magnetic pogo connector at the 6 o'clock position, AP2112K LDO, TP4056 with protection, 18650 with tabs, and the React Native app. This document only changes the force sensing subsystem, the mechanical structure that receives the punch, and the pad.

1.1 Short Answer

Recommendation: ADXL375 accelerometer + 4 flat load cells (bathroom-scale type, 50 kg) under a floating aluminum plate + a fast ADS1220 ADC. The piezo is removed and the HX711 is not used.

  • A loose strain gauge glued to a sheet? Not for these 8 weeks. A scale-type load cell already is exactly that: a machined aluminum beam with bonded gauges, calibrated, for $22 [Confirmed]. Gluing our own BF350 gauges costs the same in material, but adds bonding, curing, thermal compensation, and the risk of detachment under impacts.
  • Load cell + HX711? Not inside the pod. The HX711 delivers at most 80 samples/s: a ~10 ms punch lands on one sample or none.
  • Accelerometer only? It works and it is the simplest option, but the "force" comes from F = m·a and depends on whether the pod is on the floor, on the wall, or stacked (contradiction C2). It is kept as the fallback configuration.
  • Plates? Yes, but as structure: a 3 mm aluminum plate spreads the punch toward the cells and toward rigid stops, so neither the cells nor the electronics take the overload.

4.3 Why It Works in a Small Pod

  • The structure is oversized in the cheap parts (aluminum, ribs, stops), not in the sensors.
  • Stiffness: if each cell deflects ≈ 0.2–0.3 mm at 50 kg, the natural frequency of the plate with the pad (≈ 0.15 kg) is close to 1 kHz [Estimated], well above the frequency content of a punch (≈ 100 Hz).
  • Mounting independence: the cells measure the force between the plate and the chassis, which is the punch force minus the plate's inertia, wherever the pod is mounted.

4.4 Pad (Striking Surface): Decision

Prototype: faux leather stretched over high-density EVA (Week 6 decision, "Fixed Reflex"). It is built like a focus mitt or a drumhead: every material can be bought in Mexico without a flexible-filament printer, and the cover can be replaced in minutes. Printed TPU 95A remains as an alternative.

Conditional upgrade: cast polyether polyurethane. A Shore 60–70A skin + a Shore 20–30A core (or EVA), cast in two stages in the same mold so they bond without adhesive, and anchored to the plate through holes. Polyether resists hydrolysis (sweat and water cleaning) and impact wear better than polyester. It only goes in if a supplier confirms delivery in < 2 weeks during Week 1; it is then compared against TPU + EVA on the Week 2–3 test bench.

Discarded:

  • High-density mattress foam: 25–40 kg/m³ and ≈ 2–3 kPa at 40% compression, versus the ≈ 125–330 kPa a fist exerts [Estimated]. It bottoms out at the start of the punch, leaves the hand against the aluminum, absorbs sweat, and flattens with use.
  • Memory foam: recovers its shape slowly; unsuitable for repeated punches.
  • Sorbothane: flows under load (the signal varies with temperature) and must be imported.
  • 17–25 lb/ft³ cast PU foam: technically valid as a core, but imported with an uncertain lead time.

Bench comparison criteria: peak repeatability, punch duration, "bottoming out" signal, height loss after 1,000 punches, signal change between 20 °C and 35 °C, and comfort (3–5 people).

1.6 Force Channel Electronics

  • Bridge: the 4 half-bridge cells are wired as a full bridge (the standard scale configuration). Full-load output ≈ 1 mV/V × 3.3 V ≈ 3.3 mV [Estimated].
  • ADS1220: differential input AIN0–AIN1, PGA ×128, with a ratiometric reference taken from the bridge excitation (REFP0/REFN0) so that battery noise cancels out. Turbo mode, 2,000 SPS. Expected resolution on the order of a few newtons [Estimated; measure the noise on the bench].
  • Power saving: the ADS1220's internal low-side switch cuts the bridge excitation outside of sessions. The channel draws ≈ 3–4 mA during a session [Estimated], negligible next to the pod's ≈ 215 mA.
  • Bus: the ADS1220 and the ADXL375 share the ESP32's SPI bus with separate CS lines. The ADS1220 uses SPI mode 1 and the ADXL375 uses mode 3, so the mode is configured per device in ESP-IDF. The ADS1220's DRDY pin goes to an interrupt-capable GPIO.
  • Protection: an anti-aliasing RC filter at the input (cutoff ≈ 1 kHz [Estimated]) and a TVS diode if the cell wires are long.

1.7 Firmware and Data Fusion

  1. Contact detection (t1): whichever of two events comes first: the ADXL375 threshold interrupt or a force above a low threshold (≈ 20–30 N [Hypothesis]) on the ADS1220. This replaces the piezo.
  2. Punch force: F ≈ F_cells + m_plate · a. The plate inertia correction (≈ 0.15 kg × 100 g ≈ 150 N) is not negligible [Estimated], so it is computed with the ADXL375.
  3. Saturation: if F_cells reaches the stop level, the event is flagged saturated and the app shows the accelerometer index with the label "≥ X N".
  4. Automatic tare at the start of every session (the weight of the pad and plate, or of a stacked pod, is excluded).
  5. New event fields: force_peak_N (u16), force_impulse (u32), and the force_sat flag. The packet still fits in ≈ 50 bytes.
  6. Stacking detection: a pod on top produces a static load of ≈ 5 N per pod [Estimated] if it rests on the plate; if it rests on the chassis shoulder, the Hall sensor detects it as originally planned.

2. Communication v2

Communication Architecture v2: Pods ↔ Pods ↔ App

Supplement to the critical review (Steps 3 and 5) and to the v2 component list · Lookup date: October 8, 2026

Tags: [Confirmed] = verified in the Espressif documentation (ESP-IDF v6.1). [Estimated] = our own calculation or range. [Hypothesis] = to be validated on the test bench. [Pending] = still to be verified.

Unchanged hardware: ESP32-WROOM-32E in every pod, and a phone running the React Native app.

2.1 Decision

We keep BLE (phone ↔ one pod) + ESP-NOW (between pods). What changes is who is in charge.

In the previous version, the app directed the routine and the gateway only relayed messages. In v2, the gateway pod is the session director: it holds the full routine, decides which pod lights up and when, collects the results, and passes them to the phone. The phone only loads the routine, shows the results live, and stores the history.

Why this is the best option:

  1. The slowest link stays out of the game loop. BLE with an iPhone has a connection interval of ≥ 15 ms [Pending: confirm in Apple's accessory design guidelines], while ESP-NOW between pods takes on the order of a few ms [Estimated]. If the gateway makes the decisions, each stimulus only pays the ESP-NOW cost.
  2. The session survives anything that happens on the phone: a locked screen, the app going to the background, an incoming call, or a disconnection.
  3. Only one pod runs BLE and Wi-Fi at the same time. The ESP32 has a single 2.4 GHz radio that it time-shares between Wi-Fi and Bluetooth [Confirmed]. The other pods turn Bluetooth off.
  4. It does not depend on iOS. The phone keeps a single BLE connection, no matter how many pods there are.

2.4 How a Routine Runs

4.1 Before the Session

  1. The app connects to the gateway via BLE and sends it the full routine (blocks, duration, pods, colors, rules).
  2. The gateway distributes the routine and the configuration (thresholds, brightness) to each follower via ESP-NOW. ESP-NOW v2 supports up to 1470 bytes per packet [Confirmed]; even so, packets of ≤ 250 B are used for compatibility with v1.
  3. Clock synchronization (Section 2.5). The gateway confirms to the app that every pod is ready.

4.2 During the Session

Gateway                       Pod 3                          App
   │ decides: "pod 3, red"      │                              │
   │── STIM(id=57, t_on=T) ────►│                              │
   │                            │ lights LED at T, local t0    │
   │                            │ ... punch ... local t1       │
   │                            │ RT, peak g, force N          │
   │◄─────── EVT(seq=57, ...) ──│                              │
   │── ACK(seq=57) ────────────►│ (clears event from buffer)   │
   │ decides the next stimulus                                 │
   │── EVT via BLE notification ──────────────────────────────►│
   │◄────────────────────────────────────── ACK(seq up to 57) ─│
  • t_on scheduled ahead of time: the gateway schedules the turn-on ≈ 20 ms in the future using the synchronized clock [Hypothesis: tune on the bench]. ESP-NOW jitter is absorbed, and multi-pod patterns light up together.
  • Reaction time does not depend on the network: t0 and t1 are taken on the same pod.
  • Reactive routines ("the next pod lights up when the current one is hit") wait for the EVT, which takes a few ms over ESP-NOW [Estimated].

4.3 At the End

The gateway sends a summary, the app confirms it has every seq, and it tells the pods to return to idle.

2.5 Clock Synchronization

  • The gateway broadcasts a SYNC beacon every 500 ms with its time in µs. It also serves as a heartbeat.
  • Each follower calculates its offset and drift with a simple filter (moving average, discarding outliers).
  • Expected precision: on the order of 1 ms [Hypothesis: measure with a logic analyzer]. This is enough for simultaneous turn-ons and for ordering events.
  • ESP-NOW broadcasts cannot be encrypted [Confirmed]. That is why SYNC carries the kit_id and a truncated HMAC using the kit key.

2.6 Reliable Delivery (Zero Lost Events)

ESP-NOW's send confirmation only indicates reception at the MAC layer, not that the application received the data. Espressif recommends a custom ACK and sequence numbers to discard duplicates [Confirmed]. This is applied across two hops:

HopMechanism
Follower → gatewayEVT with seq; the gateway replies with ACK; with no ACK within ≈ 30 ms [Hypothesis], it retries (up to 5 times). The follower keeps the event until it receives the ACK
Gateway → appBLE notification; the app replies with the last contiguously received seq. The gateway keeps the events until that confirmation arrives
ReconnectionThe app requests "resend from seq = N" for each pod
DuplicatesKey (dev_id, boot_id, seq), unchanged from Step 3

2.8 If the Gateway Fails

  1. Every follower has the full routine from the start.
  2. If the gateway's SYNC is missing for > 1.5 s [Hypothesis], the followers start an election: the pod with the highest battery wins and, in a tie, the lowest MAC address. Every pod applies the same rule, so no negotiation is needed.
  3. The new gateway resumes the routine at the next stimulus, turns on its BLE, and advertises with a "gateway" flag.
  4. The app detects the drop, looks for the advertisement with that flag, and reconnects. Pending events are recovered with "resend from seq".
