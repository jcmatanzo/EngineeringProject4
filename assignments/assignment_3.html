Assignment 3: Brand Blueprint & Tech Surveillance

In this phase, we utilized generative AI to generate and evaluate brand names, conduct a phonetic trademark search (IMPI), analyze the technological ecosystem, and perform structured user interviews to validate the market need for Lumetriq.

Final Choice: Lumetriq
Lumetriq preserves the exact concept: Lumen (the light nature of the pods) + Metrics (the data axis) + Quick (the reaction speed). It is the only name that integrates the three core ideas of the product into a single word. The slight friction in pronunciation makes it highly distinctive and easier to trademark, avoiding collisions with established brands.

4. Technological Surveillance: LATAM and Global Ecosystem

Players in Mexico

  • BlazePod / Distributors: High commercialization on Mercado Libre Mexico, confirming accessible competition.
  • A-Champs ROX: Multisensory light, sound, and vibration device. Medium-high activity level in distribution.
  • FITLIGHT: Strong clinical/institutional positioning, higher complexity and cost.
  • TSENSOR (TLM): Wireless modules with acceleration, orientation, and impact. Emerging indirect competitor.

Players in LATAM

  • Lummic (Chile): Light kits, app, and programs; high activity and direct regional competitor.
  • Sonic Flash (Chile): Bluetooth lights and app. Medium-high activity.
  • t-React (Argentina): Luminous target system for coordination; medium activity.

Global Players (with impact in LATAM)

  • BlazePod (Israel): Most visible competitive benchmark; high penetration.
  • FITLIGHT (Canada): Medium-high penetration in clinics and high-performance academies.
  • Low-cost generics: Marketplace products that pressure pricing, although offering lower data quality.

Web Developer Guidelines (SEO & Copywriting)

  • Primary Domain: lumetriq.mx for the institutional site; lumetriq.app for the training platform.
  • IoT Reaction Training: Position as a connected system of lights and sensors, not just a simple game.
  • Accessible Sports Analytics in Mexico: Emphasize real-time data, Spanish app, and accessible local costs.
  • Competitive Rule: Do not claim to beat BlazePod or FITLIGHT without proof; use verifiable terms like "designed in Mexico," "modular," and "with performance data."

5. User Interviews Synthesis

Methodology Warning: The 4 interviewees (2 casual athletes, 1 coach, 1 university gym administrator) are not HIIT/functional studio owners (the original target for H1/H2). These conclusions are directional, not definitive.

1. Hypothesis Verification
  • H1 (SaaS at $1,500-2,000 MXN/month): ❌ Provisionally refuted. The administrator anchored prices to consumer apps ($60-$100 MXN), far below the hypothesis. The current cost of having no solution is zero ("everything is free" with cones and whistles).
  • H2 (LEDs and ranking distract from exhaustion): ⚠️ Partial and weak. The administrator saw value in motivation and color challenges, but no one mentioned group classes or fatigue.
  • H3 (Hardware durability): ⚪ No evidence. Pending stress tests.
2. Emerging Insight: Current Workarounds

Measurement is seen as a "vitamin," not a "painkiller." Everyone solves measurement today for free:

Interviewee How they measure today
Coach Traffic light, stopwatch, and whistle/clap dynamics.
Boxing athlete The coach, included in the $650 monthly fee.
Track and field athlete Their Garmin watch.
Administrator Free reaction speed tests.
3. Next Urgent Hypothesis

We need to reformulate H1 and test it with the correct buyer profile: "At least 3 out of 10 HIIT/functional studio owners currently spend money on circuit-direction tools, experience measurable operational pain (extra instructors, client drop-off), and will accept a real payment commitment (presale) of at least $X MXN per month."

6. Reflection and Key Takeaways

Working on this assignment highlighted how structured AI prompts can transform raw technical research into a clear, practical product plan. Using Claude and Perplexity to analyze domain availability, the IMPI database, and the regional market landscape for Lumetriq made it easy to map out where our device fits—specifically pointing to the gap left by expensive or subscription-locked competitors like BlazePod. This streamlined what would normally take days of manual tracking into a concise, well-structured market analysis.

More importantly, the user interviews grounded all of that digital research in reality. By interviewing people in our immediate circle (an athlete, a boxer, a coach, and an administrator), I realized how universal the pain point of reaction training is across different sports. Instead of relying on guesswork, hearing their actual workarounds (like using stopwatches or relying entirely on a coach's visual cues) gave us clear direction on what our hardware actually needs to survive. It proved that Lumetriq has a legitimate, practical use case right here, but also showed us that we must pivot our business model testing directly toward studio owners to validate our revenue hypotheses.
