Assignment 7: Technical & Economic Viability

Lumetriq is already desirable (Weeks 2–4) and buildable (Weeks 5–6). This week answers whether the business works: revenue model benchmarking, a D/F/S cost inventory, the real cost of the AI, and the first three steps of the economic model (unit cost, selling price, and break-even point) for the Fixed Reflex concept.

How to read this page: [Confirmed] = verified at the source on October 8, 2026. [Estimated] = our own calculation or range. [Hypothesis] = assumption to be validated with customers. [Verify] = check before deciding. Prices in MXN before VAT. Exchange rate: we keep the 18.5 MXN/USD from Week 5 (today's FIX rate is around 18.1–18.2) because it is the least favorable scenario for a BOM with imported parts. Technical baseline: PDS v1.0 and the Week 5 BOM (Ø 140 × 75 mm, BOM of $1,390 per pod at 10 units).

2. Revenue Model Benchmarking

Perplexity Prompt: Three Similar Offerings

Method: we used the week's prompt with Lumetriq as the product and boxing gyms as the segment. Prices were checked on each company's website and in recent reviews, and converted at 18.5 MXN/USD.

Similar Product or ServiceRevenue Model (Who Pays, When)Price RangeNotes: Friction and Other Stakeholders
BlazePod
floor reaction pods, no force measurement		One-time hardware payment + freemium: free app (BlazePod Go) and a monthly Pro membership with analytics and multi-athlete features.4-pod kits ≈ USD 389–534 (≈ $7,200–9,900) [Confirmed]; Pro ≈ USD 9.99/month (≈ $185) [Confirmed in reviews]Friction: reviews complain that basic features sit behind the Pro paywall after paying for the hardware. punch sensors + boxing classes		Hardware (paid upfront or financed) + a mandatory subscription: without an active membership, the sensors do not work.Equipment USD 199–899 (≈ $3,700–16,600); membership USD 39.99/month (≈ $740) [Confirmed]Friction: the mandatory subscription is perceived as a hidden cost; the real first year is ≈ USD 780–1,380. Liteboxer
wall board with 6 light-up targets and force sensors		One-time hardware payment + content subscription; its Go version gives away the sensors with a 1–2 year contract.Equipment ≈ USD 1,495–1,795 (≈ $27,700–33,200); membership ≈ USD 29.99/month (≈ $555) [Confirmed in reviews]Friction: high entry price and installation space; that is why it moved to a subsidized-hardware model. Stakeholders: connected-fitness investors; it does not sell to gyms.

Which model dominates, and why? Hardware + subscription in all three. The hardware covers manufacturing, and the subscription pays for keeping the service alive (content, app, cloud). Those that charge a mandatory subscription (FightCamp) generate more complaints; the freemium ones (BlazePod) have more non-paying users.

Which stakeholder is nobody charging? All three sell mainly to the home user. Nobody is charging the boxing gym well, even though it benefits when its members stay longer and could sell a "reaction and force assessment." Also untapped: amateur boxing schools and leagues (selection testing) and boxing equipment brands, which could sponsor challenges and leaderboards. Lumetriq goes after the gym.

3. Chosen Revenue Model

Hardware + Per-Location Subscription (B2B)

"The gym buys the pod kit and pays a monthly subscription per location; its members use the app at no cost."
LayerWhat Is ChargedWhy
1. Hardware (one-time payment)Studio Kit: 4 Fixed Reflex pods with 4 wall plates and a case, at full price (cost + margin)Subsidizing doesn't work: recovering the kit through the subscription would push it to ≈ $1,500/month (see Step 2)
2. Software (subscription)Studio: group sessions, history, gym leaderboard, AI recommendations, and reports for the coachCovers cloud, AI, and support, and it is the recurring revenue
Free for membersViewing their own history and recommendations within the gymThe anchor from the interviews was $60–100/month for an individual user: too low to sustain the product, but a useful reason for members to ask their gym for the system

Design challenge: if the pods measure and light up without paying, why renew? The value of the subscription has to be visible in class: the gym leaderboard on screen, a monthly report the owner can show members, and assessments with history. Without a subscription, the pods stay in "local reaction" mode (basic routines on the phone, with no cloud history and no AI). The paywall kicks in at the end of the first month, when there are already 4 weeks of data to lose.

5. Monthly AI Cost

Tokens Measured on the Product's Actual Prompt

The Week 5 architecture only sends the writing of the recommendation to the cloud: one call per session from the Edge Function, with aggregated metrics. This is the actual prompt that would be sent (shown here in English; the production prompt is in Spanish):

You are the LUMETRIQ training assistant, a system of light-up pods for boxing. Write a short recommendation for the athlete based on their session metrics.

Rules:
1. Use only the figures in the JSON. Do not make up data or compare with other people.
2. Write in Mexican Spanish, in the second person, with a coach's tone.
3. Maximum 120 words, in 3 parts: what improved, what needs work, and one concrete exercise for the next session.
4. Do not give medical advice. If "pain_reported" is true, recommend resting and seeing a professional.
5. If a metric is null, do not mention it.

Session metrics (JSON):
{"session_id":"s_2026_10_08_03","routine":"combos_1","duration_min":18,"total_punches":124,"valid_punches":118,
"reaction_ms":{"mean":312,"p50":298,"p90":401,"first_third":289,"last_third":341},
"force_n":{"mean":640,"max":1180,"first_third":702,"last_third":571,"saturated":0},
"per_pod":[{"pod":1,"pos":"left","reaction_ms":295,"force_n":688}, ... 4 pods],
"fatigue_pct":{"reaction":18.0,"force":-18.7},"left_right_asymmetry_pct":{"reaction":16.6,"force":17.2},
"trend_4_sessions":{"reaction_ms":[338,329,321,312],"force_n":[598,611,627,640]},
"user_goal":"improve right hook speed","pain_reported":false}
  • Input: 1,471 characters in the original Spanish prompt. At 3–4 characters per token, that is 370–490 tokens; JSON tokenizes worse than plain text and recent models use ≈ 30% more tokens, so we take 600 tokens [Estimated by Claude].
  • Output: 120 words in Spanish ≈ 160–200 tokens; we take 250 with margin.
  • Price: Claude Haiku 5.5: USD 0.10 per million input tokens and USD 0.50 per million output tokens [Confirmed, anthropic.com/pricing, Oct 8, 2026].
  • Usage: a gym with 40 sessions per day × 30 days, plus 10% retries when the verifier rejects a figure = 1,320 calls per month [Hypothesis].
Cost per call = 600 × 0.10/1,000,000 + 250 × 0.50/1,000,000
              = 0.000060 + 0.000125 = USD 0.000185

Monthly cost per gym = 0.000185 × 1,320 = USD 0.244 × 18.5 = $4.52 MXN/month
Monthly cost per pod (4 per kit)                        = $1.13 MXN/month
Monthly cost per individual user (2 per day) = 0.000185 × 66 × 18.5 = $0.23 MXN/month
ModelCost per Gym per Month% of a $499 Subscription
Haiku 5.5 (chosen)$4.520.9%
Sonnet 5.5 (USD 2 / 10 per million)$90.3518%

Conclusion: with the hybrid architecture, the AI does not threaten the margin. What keeps it cheap is the Week 5 decision to measure on the pod, analyze on the phone, and send only the writing to the cloud. If we used Sonnet "because it writes better," the AI would eat almost a fifth of the subscription. Before production, the exact value will be measured with usage.input_tokens in the API responses.

Reading the break-even:

  • A: if the team does not pay itself a salary (the student scenario), 6 gyms cover the fixed operating costs. With 10 gyms, ≈ $36,000 of contribution is left over.
  • B: paying one part-time person requires 16 gyms, more than the plan and at the limit of the Week 4 SOM (15 in year 1).
  • C: recovering development in the first year requires 30 gyms. That is not realistic: development is recovered in year 2, when the subscription is collected for a full 12 months per customer ($4,562 per gym per year) and the kit cost drops to $7,000.
  • Key sensitivity: if the kit were sold at $9,900 (BlazePod's price), the kit margin would fall to ≈ $3,034 and scenario A would require 9 gyms instead of 6.

10. Reflection and Key Takeaways

This week showed us that a good design can still be a bad business. Once we ran the numbers with the real BOM, the 6-pod price we had proposed in Week 4 turned out to be below what it costs just to manufacture the pods, something we could not see until we added overhead and contingency. We were also surprised that the AI, which looked like the riskiest cost, ends up being less than 1% of the unit cost thanks to the architectural decision to measure on the pod and send only the writing to the cloud; the fixed cloud cost and the team's hours weigh far more.

Calculating the floor and ceiling of the price made it clear how little room we have to maneuver, and that this room depends on a hypothesis we have not validated yet: how much Lumetriq is actually worth to a gym owner. For that reason, our next step is not to build more units, but to go out, ask, and try to close a pre-sale with a real deposit.
