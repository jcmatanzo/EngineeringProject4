Which model dominates, and why? Hardware + subscription in all three. The hardware covers manufacturing, and the subscription pays for keeping the service alive (content, app, cloud). Those that make the subscription mandatory (FightCamp) generate more complaints; the freemium ones (BlazePod) have more non-paying users.

Which stakeholder is nobody charging? All three sell mainly to the home user. Nobody is charging the boxing gym well, even though it benefits when its members stay longer and could sell a "reaction and force assessment." Also untapped: amateur boxing schools and leagues (selection testing) and boxing equipment brands, which could sponsor challenges and leaderboards. Lumetriq goes after the gym.

6. Monthly AI Cost

Tokens Measured on the Product's Actual Prompt

The Week 5 architecture only sends the writing of the recommendation to the cloud: one call per session from the Edge Function, with aggregated metrics. This is the actual prompt that would be sent (shown here in English; the production prompt is in Spanish):

You are the LUMETRIQ training assistant, a system of light-up pods for boxing. Write a short recommendation for the athlete based on their session metrics.

Rules:
1. Use only the figures in the JSON. Do not make up data or compare with other people.
2. Write in Mexican Spanish, in the second person, with a coach's tone.
3. Maximum 120 words, in 3 parts: what improved, what needs work, and one concrete exercise for the next session.
4. Do not give medical advice. If "pain_reported" is true, recommend resting and seeing a professional.
5. If a metric is null, do not mention it.

Session metrics (JSON):

  • Token measurement (class method: Claude was asked to count them): 1,471 characters in the original Spanish prompt. At 3–4 characters per token, that is 370–490 tokens; JSON tokenizes worse than plain text, and recent models use ≈ 30% more tokens, so we take 600 input tokens. The 120-word Spanish response is ≈ 160–200 tokens; we take 250 output tokens [Estimated by Claude].
  • Price: Claude Haiku 5.5: USD 0.10 per million input tokens and USD 0.50 per million output tokens (prompts ≤ 100K tokens) [Confirmed, claude.com/pricing, Oct 22, 2026].
  • Usage: a gym with 40 sessions per day × 30 days, plus 10% retries when the verifier rejects a figure = 1,320 calls per month [Hypothesis].
Monthly cost per user = calls/day × (input tokens × input price + output tokens × output price) × 30

7. Development Cost: Learning Cycles by Subsystem

How Much Does It Cost to Reach the First Sellable Unit?

Class method (Smith and Merritt): development cost = direct fixed + direct variable + indirect (10–20%) + contingency (25–40%), per design → build → test → learn cycle. Instead of a single generic cycle, Lumetriq is divided into 15 subsystems, each with its own cycles, and the hourly rate is based on what an engineer in Mexico earns on average.

12. Step 5: Project NPV at 15%

Does the Project Create Value in 12 Months?

NPV = −A + Σ Ft / (1 + r)^t        (monthly flows from Step 4)
Equivalent monthly rate: r = (1.15)^(1/12) − 1 = 1.171%

At 12 months, the project does not create value (−$58,906). At 24 months it does, as long as the team does not draw a salary ($129,817).
  • Why it doesn't work in 12 months: the full initial investment ($80,292) goes in at month 0, and sales start in month 3, at one gym per month. The subscription, which is the highest-margin revenue (66%), only adds up to 45 paid months in the year.
  • B almost breaks even at 24 months: its IRR (6.3%) is below 15%, so paying a salary from the start is not justified at this volume.
  • C (valuing the 2,940 engineering hours at $195) is not viable within 2 years under any realistic scenario: the full development cost is a venture capital investment, not something the first sales will pay for.

Exercises 1 to 9, Step by Step

Exercise 1 · Future Value with Quarterly Compounding

USD 45,000 at 7.5% annual interest compounded quarterly for 5 years and 6 months.

FV = P × (1 + i/m)^(m·n)    i/m = 0.075/4 = 0.01875    m·n = 4 × 5.5 = 22 quarters
FV = 45,000 × (1.01875)^22 = 45,000 × 1.504831 = USD 67,717.39

$125,000 MXN payable in 48 months, at 12% annual interest compounded monthly.

Machinery worth USD 1,000,000; cash flows of 300K / 350K / 375K / 300K + 150K salvage value; 10% rate.

Exercise 9 · IRR by Linear Interpolation

Global Solutions: USD 500,000; cash flows of 180K / 200K / 190K / 170K; 14% cost of capital.

r₁ = 14%:  NPV₁ = −500,000 + 157,894.74 + 153,893.51 + 128,244.59 + 100,653.65 = +40,686.48
r₂ = 22%:  NPV₂ = −500,000 + 147,540.98 + 134,372.48 + 104,634.31 +  76,737.84 = −36,714.38

IRR = 14% + (22% − 14%) × 40,686 / (40,686 + 36,714) = 18.21%
Exact IRR (spreadsheet IRR function)                             = 17.95%
IRR > 14%  →  viable project
  • Difference from the class presentation: the class reports NPV₁ = +40,732 and NPV₂ = −36,560. Recalculating, year 3 at 14% gives 128,245 (not 128,384), year 4 gives 100,654 (not 100,560), and at 22% years 2–4 give 134,372 / 104,634 / 76,738. The IRR barely changes (18.2%).
  • Why interpolation overestimates: the NPV-vs.-rate curve is convex, so the straight line between 14% and 22% crosses zero to the right of the true root. With two closer rates, 17% (NPV +9,300) and 18% (NPV −497), interpolation gives 17.95%, the same as the exact value.

16. Reflection and Key Takeaways

This week showed us that a good design can still be a bad business. Once we ran the numbers with the real BOM, the 6-pod package we had proposed in Week 4 turned out to be cheaper than what it costs to build, and the kit price that seemed reasonable two weeks ago ended up almost touching the cost floor as soon as the pod grew to 160 mm and the dollar went up. We were also surprised that the AI, which looked like the riskiest cost, is less than 1% of the total thanks to the decision to measure on the pod and send only the writing to the cloud. What really weighs is the team's time: valued at an engineer's salary, development turns into almost a million pesos.

The cash flow taught us the difference between being profitable and having cash. With 10 gyms, every sale leaves a margin, yet the project does not recover its investment within 12 months and needs a second year to create value. Financial math also stopped feeling abstract once we saw that the same project can be accepted or rejected depending on the discount rate. For all these reasons, our next step is not to build more pods, but to validate with gym owners how much Lumetriq is really worth to them and to secure a pre-sale.
