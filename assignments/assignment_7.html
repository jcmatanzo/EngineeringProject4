2. Revenue Model Benchmarking

Perplexity Prompt: Three Similar Offerings

Method: we used the week's prompt with Lumetriq as the product and boxing gyms as the segment. USD prices were checked on October 8, 2026, on each company's website and in recent reviews [Confirmed Oct 8]; here they are converted at 18.70 MXN/USD.

Similar Product or ServiceRevenue Model (Who Pays, When)Price RangeNotes: Friction and Other Stakeholders

Stakeholders: connected-fitness investors; it does not sell to gyms.

Which model dominates, and why? Hardware + subscription in all three. The hardware covers manufacturing, and the subscription pays for keeping the service alive (content, app, cloud). Those that make the subscription mandatory (FightCamp) generate more complaints; the freemium ones (BlazePod) have more non-paying users.

Which stakeholder is nobody charging? All three sell mainly to the home user. Nobody is charging the boxing gym well, even though it benefits when its members stay longer and could sell a "reaction and force assessment." Nobody is charging chains and franchisees with several locations either, who could use a leaderboard across locations. Lumetriq goes after the gym: it sells packages of pods with the app included.

6. Monthly AI Cost

Tokens Measured on the Product's Actual Prompt

The Week 5 architecture only sends the writing of the recommendation to the cloud: one call per session from the Edge Function, with aggregated metrics.

  • Tokens: the actual prompt has 1,471 characters; we take 600 input tokens and 250 output tokens.
  • Price: Claude Haiku 5.5: USD 0.10 per million input tokens and USD 0.50 per million output tokens (claude.com/pricing, Oct 22, 2026).

14. Financial Math Exercises

Exercises 1 to 9, Step by Step

All of them are in the Exercises tab of the spreadsheet, with formulas.

Exercise 1 · Future Value with Quarterly Compounding

USD 45,000 at 7.5% annual interest compounded quarterly for 5 years and 6 months.

FV = P × (1 + i/m)^(m·n)    i/m = 0.075/4 = 0.01875    m·n = 4 × 5.5 = 22 quarters
FV = 45,000 × (1.01875)^22 = 45,000 × 1.504831 = USD 67,717.39

Exercise 2 · Present Value of an Obligation

$125,000 MXN payable in 48 months, at 12% annual interest compounded monthly.

PV = FV / (1 + i/12)^48 = 125,000 / (1.01)^48 = 125,000 / 1.612226 = $77,532.55 MXN

Exercise 3 · Irregular Cash Flows at 9%

PV = 10,000/1.09 + 15,000/1.09³ + 8,000/1.09⁵
   = 9,174.31 + 11,582.75 + 5,199.45 = $25,956.52

Exercise 4 · Annuity: InnovaTech

Investment of USD 250,000; USD 60,000 per year for 6 years; 11% rate.

Annuity factor = [1 − (1.11)^−6] / 0.11 = 4.2305
NPV = −250,000 + 60,000 × 4.2305 = −250,000 + 253,832.27 = USD 3,832.27
IRR = 11.53% > 11%  →  viable, but with a thin margin

Exercise 5 · NPV with Variable Cash Flows

Investment of USD 50,000; cash flows of 7,500 / 12,000 / 20,000 / 25,000; 4% rate.

NPV = −50,000 + 7,500/1.04 + 12,000/1.04² + 20,000/1.04³ + 25,000/1.04⁴
    = −50,000 + 7,211.54 + 11,094.67 + 17,779.93 + 21,370.10
    = USD 7,456.24  →  viable

Exercise 6 · With Salvage Value

Machinery worth USD 1,000,000; cash flows of 300K / 350K / 375K / 300K + 150K salvage value; 10% rate.

NPV = −1,000,000 + 300,000/1.1 + 350,000/1.1² + 375,000/1.1³ + (300,000 + 150,000)/1.1⁴
    = USD 151,082.58  →  viable
Without the salvage value: USD 48,630.56. The salvage value contributes 68% of the NPV.

Exercise 7 · Project Comparison

NPV X = −50,000 + 25,000 × [1 − 1.08^−3]/0.08          = USD 14,427.42   IRR 23.4%
NPV Y = −50,000 + 10,000/1.08 + 20,000/1.08² + 45,000/1.08³ = USD 12,128.49   IRR 18.2%

X is more profitable: it has a higher NPV at 8% and a higher IRR. Y delivers most of its money only in year 3, and every peso that arrives later loses value.

Exercise 9 · IRR by Linear Interpolation

Global Solutions: USD 500,000; cash flows of 180K / 200K / 190K / 170K; 14% cost of capital.

r₁ = 14%:  NPV₁ = +40,686.48
r₂ = 22%:  NPV₂ = −36,714.38
IRR = 14% + (22% − 14%) × 40,686 / (40,686 + 36,714) = 18.21%
Exact IRR (spreadsheet IRR function)                             = 17.95%
IRR > 14%  →  viable project

Why interpolation overestimates: the NPV-vs.-rate curve is convex, so the straight line between 14% and 22% crosses zero to the right of the true root.

17. Reflection and Key Takeaways

This week showed us that a good design can still be a bad business if every peso of cost isn't watched carefully. Moving to the course's business case format boiled everything down to one question: how much do we mark up what each package costs us? At $6,000, the markup is 11.6% in the first year, and the sensitivity analysis showed that cutting the price by 10%, or a 10% rise in component costs, leaves that year with almost no profit. That is why the $1,000-per-pod cost is not a detail: it is what holds the whole model up.

We also came to understand what each peso of the price pays for. Of the $1,500 per pod, $250 is the subscription, which covers the cloud server and the AI, while employee salaries are spread across the packages we sell. We decided not to pay ourselves a salary and to come in as partners instead; with that, the project generates cash from the first month, recovers the investment in the fourth quarter of year 2, and gives the investor back 214% of what they put in.

Financial math stopped feeling abstract once we saw that the same project changes value depending on the rate used to measure it, and that even against CETES, Lumetriq comes out ahead. Our next step is to validate with gym owners that they will actually buy 3 packages, and to secure a pre-sale.
